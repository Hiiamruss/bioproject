Male Biology

The testes is where the sperms are made and stored, while the penis is the long-shaped organ that is supposed to release said gametes. Due to these sperm having a short lifespan, they will eventually be broken down and reabsorbed if they expire in the testes. Additionally, our sperms can occasionally be discarded involuntarily in sleep, commonly known as a wet dream.

Epididymis

It is a long, coiled tube resting on the back of each testicle. They store and carry the sperms created by the testicles, which also bring said sperms to maturity and readying them for fertilization.

Vas Deferens

It’s a long muscular tube, going from the epididymis into the pelvic cavity, just behind the urinary bladder. It transports the matured sperms into the urethra, where they will later be ejaculated.

Ejaculatory Ducts

These ducts are joined with the seminal vesicle ducts, which are related to the vas deferens. Said ducts move through the prostate, collecting fluid which is added to semen. Said semen is emptied into the urethra.

Reference for Ejaculatory Ducts, Seminal Vesicles, Bulbourethral Glands

Seminal Vesicles

These are sac-like pouches attached to the vas deferens near the bladder’s base. They make up 80% of the ejaculatory fluid, including fructose that assists in sperm mobility.

Bulbourethral Glands

They are pea-sized structures on the sides of the urethra, just below the prostate. They create a clear, slippery fluid that empties directly into the urethra. Said fluid lubricates your urethra and neutralizes any acids remaining from urine.

Testosterone and Puberty

In puberty, Testosterone causes pubertal changes such as growth spurts, body and hair growth, voice deepening, and growth of reproductive organs.
