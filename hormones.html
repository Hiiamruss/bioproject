Hormones Involved In Puberty

Article Made By Tiffany

Overview

Puberty is the stage when our bodies begin changing from a child's body to an adult's body. However, puberty starts in the brain. The brain sends chemical messengers called hormones to different parts of the body. These hormones control when puberty begins and helps the reproductive organs develop. The three main hormones included are the GnRH, FSH, and LH.

The Hypothalamus

The hypothalamus is a small part in the brain that helps control many body functions, including puberty. When puberty begins, the hypothalamus starts releasing hormones called GnRH (Gonadotropin-Releasing Hormone) in pulses. GnRH acts as a chemical messenger that tells the pituitary gland to release other hormones needed for puberty.

The Pituitary Gland

The pituitary gland is a small, pea-sized gland that's located below the hypothalamus. After receiving signals from the GnRH, the pituitary gland releases two hormones: FSH (Follicle-Stimulating Hormone) - helps control sperm production in males and the development of ovarian follicles containing eggs in females. LH (Luteinizing Hormone) - stimulates testosterone production in males and helps trigger ovulation and support hormone production in females. These hormones travel through the bloodstream to the reproductive organs.

How does the hormones work together?

Hypothalamus (releases GnRH) → Pituitary Gland (receives GnRH and releases FSH & LH) →Testes (males) (sperm production and testosterone release) OR ovaries (females) (follicle development and estrogen release → Physical changes (such as growth spurts, body hair, and reproductive development)

Conclusion

Conclusion Puberty is controlled by the endocrine system, which uses hormones to communicate between different parts of the body. The hypothalamus starts the process by releasing GnRH, which signals the pituitary gland to release FSH and LH. These hormones then act on the reproductive organs, causing hormonal and physical changes throughout puberty
