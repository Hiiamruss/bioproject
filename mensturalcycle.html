The Menstrual Cycle

Article Made By Karen Faith

Overview

The menstrual cycle is a 28-day hormonal loop. It lasts around 28 days on average and has four phases controlled by four main hormones: FSH, LH, estrogen, and progesterone.

Menstruation (Days 1–5)

At this stage, the uterine lining breaks down and leaves the body as menstrual blood. Estrogen and progesterone levels are low while FSH begins to stimulate new follicles to grow in the ovaries.

Follicular Phase (Days 1–13)

FSH stimulates follicles in the ovaries to grow. The growing follicle produces estrogen, which helps rebuild and thicken the uterine lining.

Ovulation (Around Day 14)

A sudden increase in LH triggers the ovary to release a mature egg. FSH also rises slightly, and the uterine lining continues to thicken in preparation for a possible pregnancy.

Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)

The empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum, which produces progesterone to maintain the uterine lining. If pregnancy doesn't occur, the corpus luteum breaks down, estrogen and progesterone levels fall, and the uterine lining sheds. This starts a new cycle.
