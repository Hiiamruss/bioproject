Female Reproductive Anatomy

The female reproductive system is divided into two primary parts: the external structures that offer protection, and the internal organs that drive the reproductive cycle. The three main internal organs are the ovaries, fallopian tubes, and uterus. Ovaries: Two glands that store egg cells and produce vital reproductive hormones. Fallopian Tubes: The narrow pathways where a released egg travels and where fertilization by sperm typically takes place. Uterus: The hollow, muscular womb where a fertilized egg implants and develops during pregnancy.

Gamete Development Before Birth

Immature cells called oogonia multiply inside the fetal ovaries. These cells develop into primary oocytes and begin their first cellular division, called meiosis I. The process pauses before birth, leaving a supply of immature egg cells inside the ovaries.

The Menstrual Cycle

From puberty onward, monthly hormonal signals stimulate a small group of ovarian follicles to develop. Usually, one dominant egg completes its first division, creating a secondary oocyte and a small cell called a polar body. During ovulation, the secondary oocyte is released from an ovary and enters a fallopian tube.

Fertilization and Implantation

The secondary oocyte begins its second division, called meiosis II, but pauses while in the fallopian tube. If a sperm cell fertilizes it, the egg completes this division. The genetic material from the egg and sperm combines as development begins. The fertilized egg starts dividing as it travels through the fallopian tube toward the uterus. It may then implant into the nutrient-rich lining of the uterus and begin developing into an embryo. If fertilization does not occur, the egg cell breaks down, and the lining of the uterus is shed during menstruation.

Physical Changes Caused by Estrogen

Estrogen is an important reproductive hormone that contributes to physical changes during puberty. Breast Development: Estrogen stimulates breast tissue development, including the duct system and supporting tissues. It works alongside other hormones to support breast growth and changes in the nipples and areolae. Maturation of Reproductive Organs: The uterus, fallopian tubes, and vagina enlarge and mature, while the external genitalia develop. Uterine Lining Growth: Estrogen stimulates the endometrium, the inner lining of the uterus, to thicken during the menstrual cycle, helping prepare the uterus for a possible pregnancy. Fat Redistribution: Estrogen contributes to changes in how the body stores fat, including around the hips, thighs, buttocks, and breasts.
